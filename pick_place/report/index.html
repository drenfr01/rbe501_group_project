Step 3 · the cause

Each servo acts like a spring: it only pushes back after it has been pulled off target.

  • The servo makes torque τ = Kp·(θ_cmd − θ).
  • To hold a load τ, it must sit τ / Kp below its command. That gap is the sag.
  • Shoulder and elbow have Kp = 20 N·m/rad, so every 1 N·m of load costs about 2.9°.

So sag grows in a straight line with cube mass. Nothing is broken; this is how a position-controlled servo works.

Show the math
τ_act = M(θ)θ̈ + c(θ,θ̇) + g(θ) + τ_passive + τ_contact payload: τ_payload = m · b(θ), b = J_vᵀ(−g_s) = 9.81 · J_v,z at rest: θ_cmd − θ = Kp⁻¹ ( g(θ) + m·b(θ) ) cube drop: Δz = b(θ)·Δθ / 9.81

J_v is the linear part of the space Jacobian at the cube's centre (Lynch & Park notation). Every term is read straight out of MuJoCo; step 4 checks that they add up.

Each dot is one episode. On the line = the formula predicts the simulator exactly. Gray × = episodes where the servo hit its torque limit.

Step 7 · the RL limit

The learned correction can move the arm at most 0.8°, but the sag is 4–5°.

  • The policy's output is scaled by 0.2 × 0.07 rad = 0.8° per step.
  • The script pulls the command back to its own target every step, so corrections can't build up.
  • Both SAC policies stay well inside that band (−0.3° and −0.1°). They only nudge.

This one design choice explains most of what follows.

Step 10 · the surprise

Randomizing usually helps a policy generalize. Here it didn't, for three reasons.

1
The error always points one way.A heavier cube always pulls the arm down. "Always push up a bit", learned at 1 kg, is right for every mass.
2
Knowing the mass doesn't help.With only 0.8° to give, the best move is "push up" at almost every mass. Random's ability to adapt has nothing to act on.
3
Random has a harder job.It must guess the mass mid-motion, and half its training cubes barely sag. It ends up hedging: smaller, gentler corrections.

Randomization's usual advantage is surviving a model that's wrong (sim-to-real). We test in the same simulator we trained in, so that advantage never shows.

Step 11 · how the policy works

The policy is a small nudge added to a scripted controller, scored by how far the task got.

  • Algorithm: Soft Actor-Critic (Stable-Baselines3), 300k steps, 5 seeds per experiment.
  • Network: actor 39 → 256 → 256 → 6 (mean and spread). Two critics score each action.
  • Only difference between the experiments: the cube mass during training.
