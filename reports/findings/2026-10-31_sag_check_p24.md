# Finding: real sag check at P = 24 (2026-10-31)

**TL;DR:** The rerun was clean: no table contact, and every final goal went through unclipped. The fitted stiffness is
**15.8 N·m/rad** on shoulder lift (95% CI 13.1–20.0) and **12.3 N·m/rad** on the elbow (10.6–14.5). That is 0.79× and
0.61× the MJCF value of 20. The fit predicts the elbow sag at the pose check's L pose exactly (2.11° vs 2.1° measured),
so the gravity sag is now calibrated. The hysteresis is not. With 3° approaches it measures only about 1.3° (full
band) on shoulder lift and 1.0° on the elbow, a third of the 4.4° the pose check measured from 30° moves. The friction
written into the calibration is therefore probably 3× too low. The likely reason is that a 3° approach is smaller
than sag plus friction band, so the "from above" sample never really comes from above.

- **Data:** `data/sag_check/2026-10-31_rbe501_follower_p24_190722/` (12 poses re-recorded higher). The
  `..._sim_p24_190707` folder is the dry run of the same poses.
- **Analysis:** `experiments/2026-10-31_sag_analysis_p24/`, calibration `data/calibration/actuator_calibration.json`,
  figure `reports/figures/real_sag.png`.
- **Procedure:** P = 24, D = 32. For every pose: raised waypoint (the L pose), then 3° above the goal on the side
  gravity pulls away from, the goal, 3° below, the goal again. 30°/s ramps, 2 s settle, 20 reads per sample.
- **Safety:** the pre-flight passed with 3 mm to spare (6° sag allowance). The first re-recording was refused
  (50 mm inside the clearance) and re-recorded higher.
- **Clipping:** LeRobot clipped 11 ramp goals, all on shoulder lift while leaving the rest pose (the joint lagged the
  ramp by more than 10°). The final goals were resent unclipped, and no sample was excluded.

## What it shows

1. **The real servo is softer than the MJCF actuator.**
   - With sag = torque / kp through zero, the fit gives 15.8 (lift) and 12.3 N·m/rad (elbow) at P = 24.
   - The sim's robustness sweep tolerates stiffness down to 0.75× (D-01, D-08). Shoulder lift at 0.79× is just
     inside that range and the elbow at 0.61× is outside it, so the current policy would not hold its accuracy.
   - From the gain sweep (error × P constant), the LeRobot default P = 16 would give about 10.5 and 8.2 N·m/rad.
2. **The fit is validated on independent data for the elbow and is roughly right for the shoulder.**
   - At the pose check's L pose (P = 24), the midpoint of the two approach directions was 2.1° on the elbow.
     The calibration predicts 2.11°.
   - Shoulder lift measured 2.8° there; the calibration predicts 1.95°.
   - Shoulder lift is not uniquely identified. A line with an intercept fits equally well (kp 37.9 plus a constant
     1.26°, r = 0.62). The poses only span 0.09–0.79 N·m, and the elbow only 0.30–0.45 N·m. A pose with a near-zero
     shoulder torque (upper arm vertical, forearm vertical) and one reaching out horizontally would separate the two
     models.
3. **The measured hysteresis depends on the size of the move.**
   - Small 3° approaches give a full band of 1.3° (lift) and 1.0° (elbow). Both are at P = 24, with the same 2 s
     settle. The pose check's 30° moves gave 4.4° on the shoulder.
   - The "from above" errors here (0.4–2.5° on the lift) are much larger than the pose check's 0.6° with gravity.
     The "from below" errors (up to 3.3°) are smaller than its 5.0° against gravity. Both directions are pulled toward
     the middle.
   - Likely cause: the arm sits at the approach point with its own sag (about 2°) and somewhere inside its friction
     band. A 3° step is then too small to reach the goal from the far side of the band, and the "above" sample
     sometimes already starts past the goal.
   - The two procedures also differ in ramp speed: 30°/s here, 15°/s in the pose check.
   - Test: rerun a few poses with an 8° approach offset (`sag_check.py run --approach-offset 8`). If the band widens toward 4.4°, the offset is the cause.
     If it stays at 1.3°, the friction really depends on the move, for example static versus kinetic friction or
     the servo's motion profile.
4. **Unloaded joints show a symmetric friction band of about ±0.75°** (pan, wrist roll, and wrist flex, which carries
   almost no load). The mean sag is 0.0°, as expected for pure friction.
5. **The early warning is optimistic.**
   - The analysis compares the *mean* sag (2.77° max) with the 2.86° limit, which is half the policy's correction
     cap, and passes.
   - The one-sided error is what the policy has to correct. It reaches 3.3° here and 5.0° in the pose check, both
     over the limit.

## Consequences for the plan

- The calibration's stiffness can be used for training now. Randomize around 0.6–0.8× MJCF (lift 15.8, elbow 12.3).
- Don't trust the calibration's friction (0.17 / 0.11 N·m) yet. Use the pose check's 4.4° band for the
  deadband/stiction model (plan step 8) until the 8° rerun settles it.
- The early warning in `analyze_sag.py` should use the worst one-sided error, not the mean.
